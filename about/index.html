Path into engineering

In 2016, during my senior year at Indiana University Southeast studying InformaticsThe practical use of computing, data, and information systems to solve real-world problems., my father died after my family had spent many years helping care for him. His health problems included an amputation and the use of a prosthetic leg. His death changed the direction of my life and strengthened my desire to work on problems that could improve people's lives.

Rather than finish the degree I was close to completing, I restarted my undergraduate path at Purdue University. I spent roughly four more years earning a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering Technology, building a foundation in mechanical design, manufacturing, experimentation, instrumentationSensors, measurement devices, and related equipment used to observe and test a physical system., controls, CADSoftware used to create and modify precise 2D drawings and 3D models of physical parts and systems., prototyping, and testing.

I later continued that path at Carnegie Mellon University, where I earned an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering. My graduate research focused on experimentally developing a temporary liver-support concept for ammonia removal in acute liver failure.

The path was not the shortest route to an engineering career, but it is why my work crosses disciplines so naturally: computing, mechanical systems, experimental research, biomedical engineering, software, and hands-on fabrication all became parts of the same problem-solving practice.

Engineering background

My work spans experimental apparatus, mechanical design, laboratory research, prototyping, instrumentation, CAD, fabrication, programming, data analysis, and technical documentation.

My strongest work tends to sit where physical systems and measurement meet: define the problem, build or modify the system, instrument it, test it, analyze the results, troubleshoot what fails, document what happened, and iterate.

I am especially interested in hands-on experimental, test, and product development work, along with validationTesting and documented evidence used to show that a product, process, or system meets its intended requirements., metrologyThe science of measurement, including how measurements are made, calibrated, and shown to be reliable., quality, manufacturing support, biomedical R&D, and related technical roles.
