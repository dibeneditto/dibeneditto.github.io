Path into engineering

In 2016, during my senior year at Indiana University Southeast studying Informatics, my father died after my family had spent many years helping care for him. His health problems included an amputation and the use of a prosthetic leg. His death changed the direction of my life and strengthened my desire to work on problems that could improve people's lives.

I left that degree unfinished and restarted my undergraduate path at Purdue University. I spent roughly four more years earning a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering Technology, building a foundation in mechanical design, manufacturing, experimentation, instrumentation, controls, CAD, prototyping, and testing.

I later continued that path at Carnegie Mellon University, where I earned an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering. My graduate research focused on experimentally developing a temporary liver-support concept for ammonia removal in acute liver failure.

The path was not the shortest route to an engineering career, but it is why my work crosses disciplines so naturally: computing, mechanical systems, experimental research, biomedical engineering, software, and hands-on fabrication all became part of the same problem-solving practice.

Engineering background

My work spans experimental apparatus, mechanical design, laboratory research, prototyping, instrumentation, CAD, fabrication, programming, data analysis, and technical documentation.

My strongest work tends to sit where physical systems and measurement meet: defining the problem, building or modifying the system, instrumenting it, testing it, analyzing the results, troubleshooting what fails, documenting what happened, and iterating.

I am especially interested in hands-on experimental, test, and product development work, along with validation, metrology, quality, manufacturing support, biomedical R&D, and related technical roles.

About this site

dibeneditto.com is a hand-built static website using semantic HTML, shared CSS, and small amounts of JavaScript. I keep the architecture deliberately simple: no large web framework, no downloaded web fonts, and no server application required for the main site.

Namecheap is the domain registrar, Cloudflare provides authoritative DNS and related network security and DDoS protection, and GitHub Pages hosts the site. The site is designed around accessibility, progressive enhancement, durable URLs, readable source code, and as few external dependencies as practical.

I do not intentionally use advertising, visitor analytics, tracking services, or tracking cookies. Some browser-based tools use local browser capabilities so settings or working data can remain on the visitor's device rather than requiring a server.
